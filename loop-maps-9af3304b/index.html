The Sixteen Loop Maps

Sixteen maps. You need one.

How To Use These

You have The Loop Map. Six stations, one turn of your own loop, worked backwards from the result because the result is the part you can already see.

The hard part was never the map. The hard part is sitting in front of a blank page with nine patterns lit and no idea which one to write about.

So this is the same map, sixteen times, already started. One for each pattern in Sixteen. Find the number that was most yours, turn to that page, and three of the six stations are already underway.

The starter lines are not your answers. They are what this pattern usually sounds like, put there so the page stops being blank. Cross them out. Write over them. Argue with them. The argument is often where your real answer lives.

One Map

Do not work through all sixteen.

These are not a program and they are not a curriculum. They are sixteen starting points so you can find your one loop faster, and one loop, mapped properly, is the whole of this work.

If you map three you will have three interesting pages. If you map one and sit with it for a week, something moves.

Pick the one that made your chest go tight when you read it. That is not a coincidence and it is not a preference. It is a signal.

Before you start

Nothing on this page is saved. Close the tab and it is gone, which is deliberate. Download your map before you leave, or write it onto the paper one.
